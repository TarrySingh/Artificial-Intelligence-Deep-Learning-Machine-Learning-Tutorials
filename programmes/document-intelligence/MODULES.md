# P02 · Module map — Document and contract intelligence for regulated operations

Every module is a lab a student *does*: a notebook they run and fill in, or a C exercise they
compile. Every module declares a compute tier and is held to it by `tools/execute.py`.

**Environment constraint, stated once.** The whole programme runs on Python 3.12 with numpy,
matplotlib, tokenizers and pytest, plus `clang` and `make`. There is no PDF library, no OCR
engine and no model API on any required path, so every corpus is generated deterministically
inside its lesson from a fixed seed. The labs below are designed within that, not around it.

## Module 1 — Field extraction you can measure: build the evaluation harness before the extractor

`lessons/P02-L01-extraction-evaluation/` · tier `cpu8` · prerequisites `T00-L01-the-8gb-track`

**The lab.** The student is handed 180 short synthetic remittance advices with gold labels,
generated inside the lesson from a fixed seed, and a deliberately mediocre stand-in extractor
with a seeded, documented error model — it reformats dates and amounts into its own house
style, drops fields, invents payment terms that were never printed, smudges a supplier name the
way a scan does, and gets a digit wrong in an amount, an invoice number or a payment term. They
then implement, in pure numpy and stdlib:

1. `normalise_value` — a per-field-type normalisation policy (money, date, id, integer, text),
   including the trap that a comma is a thousands separator in one convention and a decimal
   point in another: a lone comma is decimal only when one or two digits follow it and end the
   number.
2. `match_value` — exact, normalised and fuzzy matching, with fuzzy confined to text fields and
   its threshold inclusive. A later cell counts how many genuinely wrong amounts a fuzzy money
   matcher would wave through on this corpus.
3. `score_field` and `macro_f1` — per-field precision, recall and F1, where a wrong value counts
   as both a false positive and a false negative, and an unweighted macro average that refuses
   to let the field on every page bury the rare one.
4. `classify_cell` and `confusion_by_field_type` — five mutually exclusive labels per cell
   (correct, miss, spurious, wrong value, true negative), aggregated by field *type* under the
   matching mode it is given, so one F1 number becomes a work order.
5. `review_queue` and `apply_reviews` — a confidence-ordered routing policy under a fixed
   budget in which every cell is a candidate, a confident silence included, ties break on
   `(doc_id, field)`, and corrections land in new records rather than the ones being scored.

They finish by sweeping the budget and reading a quality/cost table: macro F1 against a random
routing control, cost per document from placeholder rates they are told to replace, and the
marginal F1 bought per unit of cost. On this corpus the yield *rises* then falls, because the
lowest-confidence cells are all misses and the next band brings in wrong values — and fixing a
wrong value is worth strictly more than fixing a miss. That is measured in the notebook, not
asserted.

**Why this is module 1 rather than module 3.** A May 2026 study of structured contract
extraction found its domain-trained model weakest on currency fields requiring normalisation,
and scored its models with no currency parsing at all (both are sourced in the
lesson's `claims.yaml`). A student who has not written the normaliser cannot tell a formatting
difference from a defect, and will spend the rest of the programme filing bugs against models
that were already right.

## Module 2 — Ingestion and layout without a PDF library

`lessons/P02-L02-layout-reading-order/` · tier `cpu8` · prerequisites `T00-L01-the-8gb-track`,
`P02-L01-extraction-evaluation`

**Lab:** reading order from geometry. The student is given synthetic pages, laid out inside the
lesson from a fixed seed, as token boxes (`text`, `x`, `y`, `w`, `h`, `page`) covering
single-column, two-column and table-bearing layouts. They implement recursive XY-cut
segmentation (`find_gaps`, `xy_cut`), a lines-first sort within each block
(`order_within_block`) and a header/footer detector that masks digits (`detect_header_footer`);
then score their reading order against the gold sequence with Kendall's tau and a block-level
boundary F1 (`kendall_tau`, `boundary_f1`), and must show a setting where the two disagree: a
page shredded into blocks keeps a perfect tau. They finish by measuring, with module 1's scorer
reproduced inside the lesson, how much field-extraction F1 moves when reading order is wrong —
naive raster order scores 0.745 against 1.000 for gold order — which is the argument for caring
about layout at all.

## Module 3 — From rules to a tagger: sequence labelling over document tokens

`lessons/P02-L03-sequence-labelling/` · tier `cpu8` · prerequisites `T00-L01-the-8gb-track`,
`P02-L01-extraction-evaluation`, `P02-L02-layout-reading-order`

**Lab:** build an averaged structured perceptron in numpy over BIO tags on a document token
stream by implementing its parts: the features (`word_shape`, `token_features` — shape, affix,
neighbouring token, line head, line position and one `head|shape` conjunction), `decode_spans`,
`sequence_scores`, `viterbi_decode` and `perceptron_update`, whose transition half is graded
because one wrong tag breaks two bigrams. The training loop and the weight averaging are given.
Train it, then score it on held-out suppliers with module 1's harness — same
scorer, different extractor, which is the whole point — and compare it field by field with a
given rule-based baseline (`improvement_table`, `fields_improved`) to identify which field types
the tagger actually improved; here it still loses to the rule on counterparty. Two deliberate
departures: the token stream is regenerated inside the lesson from a fixed seed rather than
imported from module 2, since a student bundle is one lesson directory, and the weights are
averaged once per training document rather than once per update. One unplanned result is kept
in the open: greedy decoding beats Viterbi on held-out macro F1, because the learned transitions
cut the longer held-out supplier names short.

## Module 4 — Table extraction: structure and content are two different scores

`lessons/P02-L04-table-extraction/` · tier `cpu8` · prerequisites `T00-L01-the-8gb-track`,
`P02-L01-extraction-evaluation`, `P02-L02-layout-reading-order`, `P02-L03-sequence-labelling`

**Lab:** on synthetic two-page tables laid out inside the lesson from a fixed seed rather than
imported from module 2, reconstruct rows and columns with one projection profile (`ink_runs`)
on both axes — `row_bands` on y, and columns on x read from body ink only, because a spanning
header's continuous ink hides the gaps beneath it. `cells_in_band` must cluster a band into
cells before assigning columns, and `merge_continuations` stitches a row split across a page
break, matching cells on `(col, colspan)`. Then two scorers, `content_f1` over the multiset of
cell texts and `teds_like` (built on `row_distance`, with a text-blind TEDS-Struct variant), and
a case where content is perfect while structure is wrong — every body row cut in two scores
content F1 1.000, TEDS-Struct 0.367 — and a case where the reverse is true. Ends with a rule: name the
consumer, and gate on the smallest set of scores that catches the defects that would break it.
`teds_like` is deliberately not TEDS — a two-level alignment over rows and cells, no rowspan,
clamped at zero — and the lesson says where the resemblance ends.

## Module 5 — Contract clause classification and calibrated abstention

`lessons/P02-L05-clause-classification-abstention/` · tier `cpu8` · prerequisites
`T00-L01-the-8gb-track`, `P02-L01-extraction-evaluation`

**Lab:** 6,000 synthetic clauses of six types (governing law, limitation of liability,
termination for convenience, assignment, indemnity, change of control), some of them hybrid,
smudged or truncated, deduplicated as one pool before being split into training, calibration,
test and a fourth "next quarter" split, so no clause appears twice. The byte-pair-encoding
vocabulary is trained with `tokenizers` on the training split by given code, since filling in a
`BpeTrainer` call is API lookup rather than a skill this module teaches, and the lesson trains
it twice to show that it is byte-identical. The student implements the featuriser
(`bag_of_subwords`), a multinomial logistic regression in numpy trained by full-batch gradient
descent to a stated stopping rule (`softmax`, `loss_and_gradient`, `train_softmax_regression`),
per-clause precision, recall and F1 with abstentions counted as false negatives
(`score_classes`, `macro_f1`), temperature scaling and expected calibration error
(`fit_temperature`, `expected_calibration_error`), the coverage/accuracy curve and an
abstention threshold (`coverage_accuracy_curve`, `choose_threshold`, `predict_or_abstain`),
and the policy with its hand-off (`fit_policy`, `to_review_records`). The temperature and the
threshold may come only from the calibration split, and that ordering is graded directly:
replacing the training and test splits must leave the fitted policy unchanged. Abstained
clauses go to module 1's review queue, carried into the lesson under the same names and record
format, with their real calibrated confidence.

Two results are printed rather than assumed. The fitted temperature comes out below 1, and the
student is asked what that means instead of being handed the familiar line that models are
overconfident. And temperature scaling leaves every clause's predicted type unchanged but does
reorder confidences between clauses; the lesson counts the reversed pairs. The module warns
that even an honestly fitted threshold is a point estimate chosen to just meet its accuracy
target: over 100 reshuffles of the held-out clauses it meets the target on unseen clauses only
about as often as a coin flip. It names selective classification with a guaranteed risk as the
fix this lesson does not implement.

## Module 6 — Human-in-the-loop review queues that survive contact with humans

`lessons/P02-L06-review-queues-real-reviewers/` · tier `cpu8` · prerequisites
`T00-L01-the-8gb-track`, `P02-L01-extraction-evaluation`

**Lab:** module 1's reviewer was perfect; these are not. Module 1's 180 documents and 1,080
(document, field) cells are re-created record for record from its own seeds, and three
simulated reviewers, two at tier 1 and one senior, label them with disagreement and with
fatigue that grows through a shift. The student implements raw agreement and Cohen's kappa over
the union of two reviewers' labels, including a label one of them never uses
(`agreement_table`, `raw_agreement`, `chance_agreement`, `cohens_kappa`), an SLA-aware queue
that serves arrived work earliest deadline first and reports its breaches (`sla_schedule`,
`breach_report`), a two-tier escalation rule (`escalate`), and the quality ceiling the harness
can see (`harness_ceiling`). On the day's 240 routed cells, earliest deadline first breaches no
SLA, against 26 for first-in-first-out and 59 for module 1's confidence order. Averaged over 20
simulated days, the rota chosen against a 0.05 breach-rate target is 60-item shifts: 35.6 items
an hour, no breaches, 7.5 routed cells still wrong a day, and macro F1 0.962 against the truth
where module 1's perfect reviewer gave 0.967. The rota is chosen on cells still wrong, not on
corpus macro F1, which moves by 0.007 across the sweep while cells still wrong roughly triple.

One departure from the original spec: the ceiling is not the reviewers' agreement itself. Their
kappa is 0.561; a perfect system scored against gold one of them wrote reaches about
`sqrt(kappa)`, 0.749, against a measured 0.737 between the simulation's truth and reviewer A,
and a perfect extractor scored against A's gold reaches macro F1 0.976, not 1.000. The lesson
grades that form, states its assumption of independent mistakes, and shows the ceiling rising
to 0.935 when the second reviewer shares A's mistakes. Exercise 3's hint warns about the
readiness test that makes the scheduler loop forever, since the grader has no per-test timeout.

## Module 7 — Error analysis and slice discovery: is that regression real?

`lessons/P02-L07-slice-discovery/` · tier `cpu8` · prerequisites `T00-L01-the-8gb-track`,
`P02-L01-extraction-evaluation`

**Lab:** 1,600 synthetic documents carrying module 1's six fields and five attributes (vendor,
layout family, language, page count, scan quality), with two given extractor versions scored by
module 1's harness, carried into the lesson. The student implements slice discovery over single
attributes and two-attribute crosses above a minimum size (`find_slices`), module 1's macro F1
computed from per-document counts for every replicate at once (`macro_f1_from_counts`), a
paired bootstrap over documents for the change in a slice's macro F1, with a percentile
interval and a one-sided p-value that is never zero (`paired_bootstrap`), Holm's step-down and
Benjamini-Hochberg's step-up adjustments (`holm_adjust`, `bh_adjust`), the sample size a slice
needs before it may block a release (`required_slice_size`), and the release decision
(`release_decision`), in which a slice below that size is "too small to say" whatever its
p-value. On this release only the fax slice blocks; the size gate is what stops a 13-document
vendor slice (change −0.120, Holm-adjusted p 0.033) from blocking as well.

The regression is planted in one place, digit fields on faxed documents, and every slice
inherits a diluted share of it. The lesson re-runs the whole pipeline on 20 null releases, one
retrain of v1 against another, and counts how often each rule blocks when nothing changed: any
drop 20 times, a drop beyond the margin 15, an uncorrected p-value 14, and Holm, with or without
the size gate, never. It also discloses that the seeds were picked so that this one release
shows every failure the lesson teaches, the Holm-flagged small slice among them. Depth-2 crosses
are implemented and graded but not used for the gate: 253 slices raise the required size from
162 to 214 documents and put the smallest Holm-adjusted p-value that 2,000 bootstrap replicates
can produce at 0.1264, above 0.05, so a search that deep could not block anything.

## Module 8 — Drift monitoring for a live document pipeline

`lessons/P02-L08-pipeline-drift-monitor/` · tier `cpu8` · prerequisites `T00-L01-the-8gb-track`,
`P02-L01-extraction-evaluation`

**Lab:** a synthetic replay year of 364 days, 200 documents a day and a random audit of 25,
carrying a vendor template change, a seasonal mix shift and one real model regression. The
student implements the population stability index and KL divergence over field-value and
confidence distributions, on edges cut once on a baseline quarter and floored on both sides so
that `KL(a‖e) + KL(e‖a) = PSI` exactly (`population_stability_index`, `kl_divergence`,
`fit_edges`, `drift_scores`); the audited defect rate as a daily quality proxy, from module 1's
miss, spurious and wrong-value labels over reviewed cells only (`daily_defect_rate`); an upper
one-sided CUSUM with reset (`cusum`); the most sensitive threshold whose false-page rate on 200
simulated no-change years stays within a budget of one false page a quarter
(`false_pages_per_quarter`, `choose_threshold`); and a rule that names a change by which
distribution moved and whether the audited quality moved (`triage`). The chosen threshold is
printed beside the handbook's rules of thumb, h = 4 and 5, with the false pages each costs and
the documents each lets through before it pages.

Whether the monitor paged *for* an event is decided by counterfactual, not by calendar: the
year is generated so that a copy with one event switched off differs from the log only by that
event. On this replay the regression pages the day after it goes live, neither harmless change
causes a page, and there are 0 unexplained pages in 105 event-free monitored days where the
budget allowed about 1.2; paging on PSI above 0.25 does the reverse, paging for both harmless
changes and never for the regression. Two warnings close the lab. The no-change years are
resampled from the baseline quarter, because a real pipeline has no generator to draw from, so
their tail is thinner than reality's. And a rolling baseline erases the template change within
four weekly reports (confidence PSI from 0.3769 to 0.0053), which makes it invisible, not
resolved.

## Module 9 — Cost per document, and the routing budget that sets it

`lessons/P02-L09-cost-routing-budget/` · tier `cpu8` · prerequisites `T00-L01-the-8gb-track`,
`P02-L01-extraction-evaluation`, `P02-L03-sequence-labelling`,
`P02-L06-review-queues-real-reviewers`, `P02-L07-slice-discovery`

**Lab:** a three-tier router (cheap extractor, expensive extractor, human review) priced from
named placeholders: module 1's machine cost and reviewer rate, module 6's reviewing pace, and a
per-page price for the expensive tier. It routes an eight-segment traffic fixture whose per-tier
accuracies are shaped on what modules 1–4 and 6–8 measured, each segment labelled with the
module it mirrors. That is the departure from the spec: a notebook runs alone and cannot import
another lesson's results, so these are fixtures, not the student's own measurements. The
student implements cost per document and field accuracy of a routing plan as volume-weighted
means over segments (`evaluate_plan`), an exhaustive sweep and the most accurate plan within a
cap (`sweep_plans`, `best_within_budget`), the Pareto frontier as a staircase rather than a
convex hull (`pareto_frontier`), a greedy marginal-yield allocator that records its path
(`greedy_allocation`), a segment mix on which the sweep beats greedy, graded against reference
implementations so that it must be a genuine counterexample (`greedy_counterexample`), and the
figures of a recommendation paragraph whose wording is given and adds no number of its own
(`recommend`).

Of 6,561 plans, 736 fit the placeholder cap of 0.30 per document. The sweep's plan costs 0.2920
per document; greedy, held to the same cap, leaves 2,436 more fields wrong a month; and the next
step up the 48-plan frontier costs 1.74 per wrong field avoided. Greedy matches the sweep at 213
of 241 caps, at all 15 of its own breakpoints and on 130 of 150 random six-segment mixes, so the
module does not call it unreliable: it measures where greedy is exact and has the student
construct a case where it is not. Every price is a placeholder the student is told to replace
before anyone signs, and swapping module 6's reviewing pace for module 1's placeholder alone
moves one of the eight segments to another tier.

## Module 10 — The scanner in C: fixed memory over an unbounded export

`lessons/P02-L10-streaming-scanner-in-c/` · tier `cpu8` · prerequisites `T00-L01-the-8gb-track`,
`P02-L01-extraction-evaluation`

**Lab:** implement, in `lesson.c`, a streaming field scanner in constant memory over a synthetic
export the program generates from a fixed seed: `field_push` (a fixed buffer, overflow counted
per field, not per byte), `field_complete`, `scan_chunk` (quoted fields, escaped quotes and
embedded newlines, with counters that must not depend on where the stream was split — graded
down to one-byte chunks) and `scan_finish` (a truncated final record versus a legal one with no
trailing newline). It is built with `make` and graded against a Python oracle on identical
bytes, and on peak RSS staying flat as the export grows. They measure throughput and peak RSS
against that Python scanner and learn where each factor comes from: C buys the throughput, the
chunk loop the memory. Two departures: the Python scanner is the one given in this lesson,
since module 1 has no scanner, and the export stops short of multiple gigabytes, because the flat
memory line is already unambiguous. Its common-mistakes section tells students to run
`make clean` after moving or rebuilding their checkout, as `QUALITY.md` requires.

## Module 11 — Capstone: the conformity pack

`lessons/P02-L11-capstone-conformity-pack/` · tier `cpu8` · prerequisites
`T00-L01-the-8gb-track`, `P02-L01-extraction-evaluation`, `P02-L02-layout-reading-order`,
`P02-L03-sequence-labelling`, `P02-L04-table-extraction`,
`P02-L05-clause-classification-abstention`, `P02-L06-review-queues-real-reviewers`,
`P02-L07-slice-discovery`, `P02-L08-pipeline-drift-monitor`, `P02-L09-cost-routing-budget`,
`P02-L10-streaming-scanner-in-c`

**Lab:** the harness, the residual-by-class analysis, the drift alarm, the escalation policy
and the cost model from the earlier modules are carried in, given, and assembled into one
evidence pack for a synthetic pipeline of 240 documents; the seven graded exercises are the
validator that checks it. The student implements a reproduction gate that extracts every figure
from the pack's text and requires each one to reproduce, within the precision the text itself
claims, from a computed evidence bundle (`extract_figures`, `computed_values`, `trace_gate`);
three provenance checks: the residual-by-class table recomputed from the raw records on the
split the pack claims (`check_residual_provenance`), every cost figure presented as measured
cited to a matching entry in a measurement registry (`check_cost_provenance`), and an alarm
threshold calibrated strictly before the day it is credited with catching
(`check_threshold_timing`); and a validator that refuses the pack unless all four pass, graded
as an AND for every check rather than a majority (`validate_pack`). Module 10's C scanner is a
prerequisite by programme order but is not carried into the pack.

Four defects are planted, each built to be caught by exactly one check: a residual table
computed on the training split and labelled as the evaluation split, a derived cost figure
labelled measured with no source, a threshold calibrated through day 258 for an alarm on day
253, and a typed commentary figure that nothing computed. The lesson plants each alone in a
clean pack and prints which check catches it. The reproduction gate alone catches one of the
four, because the other three figures are correct for the data the preparer actually used and
trace against the preparer's own evidence bundle. The module warns that repairs which only make
a validator quieter (widening a tolerance, loosening the timing comparison by one step, dropping
a check from the combination) leave the pack no more honest, and the rubric fails each of them.
