# Module map — EU AI Act conformity engineering

Every module is `cpu8`: 8 GiB, 2 vCPU, no GPU, no network, standard library plus numpy where a
module needs it. Every module is a notebook with scaffolded stubs, public checks, an autograded
rubric with partial credit, a worked solution and a self-check. Every module ends with an
artefact, not a summary.

The spine is the evidence table from the prerequisite lesson
`lessons/T10-L01-ai-act-conformity-pack`. Modules 1 and 3 to 8 each fill evidence ids that
lesson defines, and the capstone re-verifies six of them, so the pack stays machine-checkable
as the programme grows. Module 2 fills none: its classification record is the input module 8
reads to choose the route.

## 1. Article 12 logging and traceability

`lessons/P01-L01-article-12-logging` · prerequisites `T10-L01-ai-act-conformity-pack`

**The lab.** The student implements, from stubs, five things and then runs an inspection over
the log they built:

1. `canonical_bytes()` — one deterministic serialisation, because hashing a dict is hashing
   *one* serialisation of it.
2. `append_event()` — an append-only chained entry whose SHA-256 digest covers its sequence
   number, its predecessor's hash and its event, so an edit, a reorder and a deletion each
   move it.
3. `verify_chain()` — names the first entry that breaks and why, then checks the head against
   an anchor published outside the log. A whole-tail rewrite is internally flawless; only the
   anchor catches it, and the exercise makes the student see that happen.
4. `retention_status()` — what may be purged, what must be kept, and which sequence numbers
   have already gone. The Article 19 and 26(6) six-month floor is a floor, not a ceiling, and
   the boundary is inclusive.
5. `reconstruct_decision()` and `completeness_report()` — rebuild one inference decision in
   sequence order, pull in the deployment event it depends on, and report which required
   fields it is missing.

**What makes it not a lecture.** The event stream contains a clock that stepped backwards
mid-decision, so ordering by timestamp visibly reverses a decision; a retention boundary two
entries land exactly on; and a decision whose session never closed. The rubric grades the
verifier's reason as well as its verdict: a gap must be blamed on the numbering, a broken link
on `prev_hash` and an edited event on `entry_hash`, because each sends an inspector somewhere
different.

**The honesty it teaches.** Article 12(3)'s four-item minimum is written for the Annex III
point 1(a) remote biometric identification systems. The lesson's fictional system scores
credit applications, so the required-field list it checks is labelled, field by field, with
where it came from — including the fields that are the lesson's own modelling choice rather
than Article 12(3)'s minimum. A field list you cannot argue with is a field list nobody checked.

**Data.** Nothing is read or downloaded: the event stream is constructed inside `lesson.py`
against a fixed as-of date, and its match scores come from a seeded numpy generator.

## 2. Risk classification as a decision procedure

`lessons/P01-L02-risk-classification` · prerequisites `T10-L01-ai-act-conformity-pack`,
`P01-L01-article-12-logging`

**The lab.** T10-L01 gave the student a `classify()` that reads flags off a dictionary. This
module makes them earn those flags. They implement an **interview**, `ask_tier()`: an ordered
decision procedure that stops at the first question that decides a tier and records each
answer *and the question that produced it*. Around it sit `obligations_for()`, the obligations
a tier implies and the date each binds; `classification_record()`, sealed with module 1's
canonical bytes; `diff_records()`, which reports the obligations that appeared or vanished
across a system change and on what date; and `disagreement_report()`, the earliest question a
panel of analysts split on. Where this spec asked for a reclassification over two system
descriptions, the lesson diffs two sealed records, because records are what a pack holds six
months apart.

The graded subtlety is Article 6(3). An Annex III system is **not** high-risk where it does not
pose a significant risk of harm to health, safety or fundamental rights *and* meets one of four
conditions — a narrow procedural task, improving a previously completed human activity,
detecting decision patterns without replacing the prior human assessment, or a preparatory task
— but a system that performs **profiling of natural persons is always high-risk regardless**,
and a provider claiming the derogation must document its assessment before placing the system
on the market. The student implements the derogation in `derogation_assessment()`, the
profiling override that trumps it, and the documentation duty that comes with claiming it. The
rubric fails an implementation that grants the derogation to a profiling system, even one with
perfect paperwork, and one that grants it without a justification.

**Data.** Fictional systems and analyst intake forms, authored in `lesson.py` as synthetic.

**Artefact:** a classification record with its own question trail, appendable to the module 1
log.

## 3. Annex IV technical documentation, generated rather than written

`lessons/P01-L03-annex-iv-documentation` · prerequisites `T10-L01-ai-act-conformity-pack`,
`P01-L01-article-12-logging`

**The lab.** Annex IV lists what the Article 11 technical documentation must contain: general
description; elements and development process; monitoring, functioning and control;
performance metrics; the risk management system; changes through the lifecycle; harmonised
standards applied; the EU declaration of conformity; and the post-market performance
evaluation system.

The student builds a generator over *typed sources* — a model registry, a dataset manifest, a
metrics file and the rest — that refuses to emit a section it cannot source: `field_value()`
and `is_sourced()`; `check_section()`, which tells an absent field from a present, blank one;
`provenance_stamp()`; `render_document()`, which names every path each refusal waits for; and
`staleness_report()`, which re-checks every stamp. Where this spec weighed modification time
and content hash together, the digest decides and the timestamp is only a diagnostic. The
module 1 log appears as an `event_log_design` artefact rather than being read directly.

The trap the rubric sets: a section that renders successfully from an *empty* source. A
document generator that happily produces "Performance metrics: —" is worse than one that
fails, because it produces a green pack. The sources carry a blank field, an absent one, a
blank template row and a missing declaration, and the honest output reports
`5 of 9 sections can be sourced today`.

**Data.** Every source artefact is synthetic, built from fixed literals in `lesson.py`.

**Artefact:** an Annex IV document with a per-section provenance table and a staleness verdict.

## 4. Data governance you can test

`lessons/P01-L04-data-governance` · prerequisites `T10-L01-ai-act-conformity-pack`,
`P01-L01-article-12-logging`

**The lab.** Article 10 asks for training, validation and test data that are relevant,
sufficiently representative and, to the best extent possible, free of errors and complete in
view of the intended purpose — plus an examination for possible biases.

The student implements two power tools, `min_detectable_share()` and
`min_detectable_difference()`, then five checks over a small synthetic tabular dataset, in
numpy: `near_key()` and `leakage_report()` (duplicates across the splits, and an identifier
that predicts the target); `coverage_report()` against a declared intended population, with
`gap_report()` giving the Article 10(2)(h) remedy per gap; `drift_report()` between training
and test; `provenance_report()`, a licensed source per split and an approved method per label;
and `bias_probe()`, subgroup error rates under a minimum-support rule. The bias probe and the
gap report go beyond this spec's four tests.

Each test returns a verdict *and* a power statement: with this sample size, how small an
effect could this test have detected? A bias examination that cannot say what it would have
missed is not an examination. The probe's loudest row, `a 100% error rate on three people`,
must be reported as insufficient support rather than the system's worst disparity.

**Data.** A synthetic loan-application table generated in `lesson.py` from one seeded numpy
generator, its defects planted deliberately.

**Artefact:** a data governance record in which every verdict carries its power statement, plus
the Article 10(2)(h) gap report.

## 5. Human oversight as a measurable property

`lessons/P01-L05-human-oversight` · prerequisites `T10-L01-ai-act-conformity-pack`,
`P01-L01-article-12-logging`

**The lab.** Article 14 requires high-risk systems to be designed so they can be effectively
overseen by natural persons, and Article 14(5) requires, for certain systems, that no action
be taken on the basis of an identification unless separately verified and confirmed by at
least two natural persons.

The student reads the `verifier_ids` field module 1's log records, over a generated log in that
shape. `decision_view()` folds it into one row per decision in sequence order; on that view they
compute `override_rates()`, keyed on the model's recommendation rather than the outcome;
`time_to_decision()`, a proxy that tells a backwards clock from a reviewer who was too fast;
`four_eyes_report()`, distinct natural persons on the decisions the two-person rule reaches;
and `automation_bias()`, the override rate against the model's own confidence, judged by each
reviewer's own minimum detectable difference. Where this spec had one automation-bias number,
the lesson has a four-rung status, because one number cannot tell justified trust from a
reviewer who has stopped reading. The reviewer it flags does not have the highest headline
rate; split by confidence, `her high-confidence override rate is 4.0%`.

Then they implement the control: `oversight_gate()` blocks an action for every reason it fails,
not just the first, and `gate_report()` runs it against the log to find the decisions that
would have been blocked.

**Data.** A synthetic event log generated in `lesson.py` from one seeded numpy generator.

**Artefact:** an oversight plan with measured baselines, and a gate that enforces it.

## 6. Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity evidence

`lessons/P01-L06-accuracy-robustness-security` · prerequisites
`T10-L01-ai-act-conformity-pack`, `P01-L01-article-12-logging`, `P01-L04-data-governance`

**The lab.** Article 15 requires an appropriate level of accuracy, robustness and
cybersecurity, and consistent performance in those respects throughout the lifecycle — and it
requires the declared accuracy metrics to be stated in the instructions for use.

Three parts, all numpy. **Accuracy:** `bootstrap_ci()`, a paired percentile bootstrap, and
`declare()`, which refuses a bare point and declares the defensible end of the interval,
because a declared accuracy without one cannot be falsified or defended:
`accuracy at least 0.8725 on 1200 held-out applications`. **Robustness:** `seal_floor()` puts
the floor in a hash-chained ledger before the metric is measured, `perturbation_curve()` sweeps
missing fields, unit changes, sensor noise and a stale feature, and `crossing_severity()`
reports where the declared lower bound crosses the floor, in each family's own severity unit.
**Cybersecurity:** `poison_scan()`, a neighbourhood scan of upload batches under module 4's
support floor; `extraction_risk()`, the query budget a surrogate needs, on the
attacker-favourable bound; and `residual_risk()`, which names each risk bounded or unbounded.
A ninth exercise the spec did not name, `unbounded_numbers()`, audits the report for any number
with no interval.

The rubric fails any declaration of a bare point, and any crossing report whose floor was
sealed after a point of the curve it judges.

**Data.** Synthetic applicants, upload-batch label flips and attacker queries, drawn in
`lesson.py` from fixed-seed numpy generators. `claims.yaml` names UCI Statlog (German Credit
Data), CC BY 4.0, as an optional off-path extension that no exercise or rubric case touches.

**Artefact:** an Article 15 evidence report whose every number carries its uncertainty.

## 7. Post-market monitoring and serious incidents

`lessons/P01-L07-post-market-monitoring` · prerequisites `T10-L01-ai-act-conformity-pack`,
`P01-L01-article-12-logging`

**The lab.** Article 72 requires providers to establish and document a post-market monitoring
system that actively and systematically collects, documents and analyses relevant data on
performance — data that may be provided by deployers or collected from other sources.
Article 73 requires serious incidents to be reported.

The student builds the feed over a sequence-numbered provider log: `control_limits()` from a
declared baseline window, and `windowed_monitor()`, which cuts windows on sequence numbers
rather than timestamps and confirms an excursion before it pages; module 1's rule returns at
scale in a batch a broken exporter flushed late. The **deployer ingest** path is
`estimate_clock_offset()`, a median with its spread, and `merge_deployer_feed()`, which does not
count the overlap twice. The **incident classifier**, `classify_incident()` and
`incident_log()`, starts the Article 73 clock at awareness, not at occurrence. The lesson
monitors its own metric, the share of decisions later found wrong, rather than module 6's, and
uses tumbling windows where this spec said rolling, because overlapping windows count one
excursion many times.

The graded subtlety is the difference between a drifting metric and an incident, and the cost
of getting it wrong in each direction: a monitor that pages on every wobble is switched off
within a month, and one that never pages is indistinguishable from no monitor.
`monitor_costs()` prices both, plus a term this spec did not model: enough false pages mute the
monitor. On the lesson's feed the textbook default loses: `cheapest k on this feed: 2.0 at 400`.

**Data.** A synthetic provider log, deployer feed and incident candidates built in `lesson.py`.

**Artefact:** a monitoring plan with computed control limits, and an incident log with clocks.

## 8. The conformity assessment route

`lessons/P01-L08-conformity-assessment-route` · prerequisites
`T10-L01-ai-act-conformity-pack`, `P01-L01-article-12-logging`, `P01-L02-risk-classification`

**The lab.** Article 43 sets out which conformity assessment procedure applies. For the
Annex III **point 1** systems (biometrics) the provider chooses between internal control under
Annex VI and a notified-body assessment of the quality management system and technical
documentation under Annex VII, but only where the harmonised standards were applied in full;
any of Article 43(1)'s standards gaps makes Annex VII compulsory. For Annex III **points 2 to
8** — including critical infrastructure, employment and essential services such as
creditworthiness — it is Annex VI internal control, with no notified body involved. Annex I
systems fold into their product's own procedure instead.

The student implements `annex_iii_point()`, which reads the point from the module 2 record and
leaves the route undetermined when it cannot; `standards_gap()`; and `assessment_route()`.
`readiness()` checks that the route's evidence is present, in date and traceable, reusing
module 1's `completeness_report()` unchanged rather than inventing a second checker.
`declaration_of_conformity()` (Article 47) refuses while any required item is missing, and
`ce_marking_record()` (Article 48) refuses without a declaration, or where a digital marking
cannot easily be accessed — a refusal this spec did not ask for.

The trap: a substantial modification resets the route. The student implements
`is_substantial_modification()` and `modification_history()` over a change log; the route
resets only for a substantial change the assessment on file does not cover, where this spec
reset it for any. The rubric fails a declaration emitted for a system whose modification should
have sent it back through assessment, and the lesson prints the point for that system:
`A pack with nothing missing, and no declaration.`

**Data.** Fictional records, change logs and evidence packs declared as literals in `lesson.py`.

**Artefact:** a route decision, a readiness verdict, and a declaration that cannot be emitted
while the pack is incomplete.

## 9. The pack under inspection (capstone)

`lessons/P01-L09-capstone-pack-under-inspection` · prerequisites
`T10-L01-ai-act-conformity-pack`, `P01-L01-article-12-logging`, `P01-L02-risk-classification`,
`P01-L03-annex-iv-documentation`, `P01-L04-data-governance`, `P01-L05-human-oversight`,
`P01-L06-accuracy-robustness-security`, `P01-L07-post-market-monitoring`,
`P01-L08-conformity-assessment-route`

**The lab.** A complete evidence pack for a fictional system is handed to the student —
already assembled, already attested green by its provider — along with an inspector's question
list. Six of the artefacts are wrong in ways the earlier modules taught them to detect: a log
with a purged window, a document section generated from an empty source, an accuracy figure
with no interval, an oversight record where one reviewer approved 400 cases in an hour, a
declaration emitted after a substantial modification, and a monitoring feed whose control
limits were recomputed after the breach.

The toolkit is handed back rather than re-derived — `verify_chain()` from module 1,
`check_section()` from module 3, `declare()` and `bootstrap_ci()` from module 6,
`control_limits()` and `windowed_monitor()` from module 7, `is_substantial_modification()` from
module 8 — and the six checks are given too. Five call into that toolkit; the oversight check
reads module 5's records for throughput without calling a module 5 function. The student
implements `run_suite()`, which reports a check nobody ran as unverified, not passed;
`inspect_pack()`; `evidence_delta()`, `suite_delta()` and `unsupported_values()`,
three independent measures of what a repair did, none trusting the repairer's own account;
`silencing_audit()`, repaired versus silenced versus unfixed; `plan_repairs()`, the fix as
operations against the pack; and `inspection_report()`. The rubric grades the findings, the
fixes, and — the part that matters — whether any of their "fixes" merely silenced a check. The
lesson prints two packs both `6/6 checks green`, one accepted and one rejected.

**Data.** One synthetic pack generated in `lesson.py` from one seeded numpy generator.

**Artefact:** an inspection report and a repaired pack, both reproducible from the toolkit the
lesson hands back.
