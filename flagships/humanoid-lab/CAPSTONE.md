# F15 Capstone · Walk a measured distance without falling

**The task.** Make the planar biped from `F15-L05` walk forwards. Not "produce a gait that
looks like walking" — cover a measured distance, on a measured energy budget, without falling,
and keep doing it when the machine is not quite the machine you tuned on.

**Why it is graded under randomisation.** A gait tuned against one parameter set scores 100%
against that parameter set by construction; that is the definition of the thing you tuned on,
not evidence about anything. `F15-L08` makes you measure this on a balancer. The capstone
applies the same rule to the walker: your headline score comes from parameters your search
never saw.

---

## What you submit

One directory, `capstone/`, containing exactly:

```
capstone/
  policy.py        your controller — the only file the harness imports
  REPORT.md        600 words maximum, structure below
  figures/         optional, at most 3 PNGs generated by your own code
```

Nothing else. No notebook, no data dump, no vendored copy of the model. The harness supplies
the model.

### `policy.py` — the contract

The harness imports your module and calls exactly three names. Anything else in the file is
yours.

```python
NAME: str          # what you want on the leaderboard, 40 characters or fewer

def make_policy() -> object:
    """Return whatever object your controller needs: gains, a table, a small network.

    Called ONCE per grading run, before any episode. Must not read the model, the
    conditions, or any file outside your own directory. Deterministic: two calls in the
    same interpreter must return equivalent objects.
    """

def act(policy, observation: "np.ndarray", t: float) -> "np.ndarray":
    """Return the control vector for this step: shape (4,), the four joint targets.

    `observation` is the 14-number state the harness hands you (see below). `t` is elapsed
    SIMULATED seconds, so a periodic gait can use it as phase.

    Called once per control step. Must be pure with respect to the world: the only state you
    may carry between calls is inside `policy`. Returning a value outside the model's
    ctrlrange is allowed — MuJoCo will clamp it, exactly as in F15-L08 — but a submission
    that spends most of its episode clamped will say so in its saturation score.
    """
```

**The observation, in order** (14 numbers, all in SI units, all readable from `mjData`):

| index | quantity |
|---|---|
| 0–6 | `qpos` — hip x, hip height, torso pitch, then the four joint angles |
| 7–13 | `qvel` — the matching velocities |

**What you may not do.** Read `mjModel` (you do not get to know the mass you are being graded
against). Import anything outside the standard library, `numpy` and `mujoco`. Write files
during an episode. Call the harness's own functions. Sleep, thread, or spawn a process. The
harness runs with no network and will fail your submission if it attempts one.

## The measurement script contract

The harness is `measure_walk.py` and you are given it — grading you against a script you
cannot run yourself would be a trick, not an assessment. It exposes one function, and your
`REPORT.md` numbers must be the ones it prints.

```python
def measure_walk(policy_module, conditions: list, seconds: float = 8.0) -> dict:
    """Run one episode per condition and return the aggregate scorecard.

    Returns a dict with exactly these keys:
      "distance_m"            float, MEAN forward hip travel over surviving episodes
      "survival_rate"         float in [0, 1], episodes that never fell
      "cost_of_transport"     float, mean dimensionless CoT over surviving episodes
      "saturated_fraction"    float in [0, 1], mean fraction of steps against a torque limit
      "episodes"              list of per-condition dicts, same keys plus "fell_at_step"
    """
```

Fixed by the harness, not by you:

- **Horizon** — 8.0 simulated seconds per episode.
- **Fallen** — hip height below 0.55 m, or torso pitch beyond 0.6 rad, at any step. A fallen
  episode contributes to `survival_rate` and to nothing else.
- **Distance** — `qpos[0]` at the final step minus at the first. Backwards counts as negative;
  there is no credit for travelling in the wrong direction.
- **Cost of transport** — the dimensionless CoT you built in `F15-L05`: total actuator work
  divided by (total mass × g × distance). Undefined for a zero-distance episode, which is
  therefore scored as a non-finish.
- **Conditions** — generated by `sample_conditions` from `F15-L08`, over the walker's own
  spec. You are given the spec and the *development* seed. **You are not given the grading
  seed.**

### Run it yourself, before you submit

```bash
# from the repository root
.venv/bin/python flagships/humanoid-lab/capstone/measure_walk.py \
    --policy flagships/humanoid-lab/capstone/policy.py --seed dev --episodes 24
```

A submission whose reported numbers do not reproduce under the grading seed, within the noise
the harness reports, scores zero on Honesty. That is the one section with no partial credit.

## Rubric — 100 points

Graded by running the harness, then reading `REPORT.md`. Partial credit throughout, except
where stated.

### 1. It walks — 30 points

| points | held-out mean distance |
|---|---|
| 30 | ≥ 4.0 m |
| 24 | ≥ 3.0 m |
| 18 | ≥ 2.0 m |
| 12 | ≥ 1.0 m |
| 6 | ≥ 0.25 m, forwards |
| 0 | it does not travel forwards |

### 2. It stays up — 25 points

`survival_rate` over 40 held-out conditions, scored linearly: `round(25 × survival_rate)`.
Survive every condition and it is 25; survive three quarters and it is 19.

### 3. It survives parameters it never saw — 20 points

The capstone's real question. The harness scores your policy twice: on the development
conditions you had, and on the held-out grading conditions you did not.

| points | outcome |
|---|---|
| 20 | held-out survival is within 10 points of development survival |
| 14 | within 25 points |
| 8 | within 40 points |
| 0 | held-out survival collapses by more than 40 points |

A policy that scores 100% on development and 40% on held-out has not been shown to work. It
has been shown to have been tuned. This section exists to price that difference, and it is why
a slightly worse development score is often the better submission.

### 4. It is not thrashing — 10 points

| points | mean `saturated_fraction` |
|---|---|
| 10 | ≤ 0.25 |
| 7 | ≤ 0.50 |
| 4 | ≤ 0.75 |
| 0 | above 0.75 |

A gait that spends its whole life against the torque limit has no margin left for the next
disturbance. This is the cheapest early warning you get, and `F15-L08` shows it moving before
survival does.

### 5. It is efficient — 10 points

Cost of transport on surviving held-out episodes, relative to the cohort. Top quartile 10,
second 7, third 4, bottom 0. Scored relative because an absolute CoT target would be a number
typed by a course author rather than measured, which `QUALITY.md` gate 12 forbids.

### 6. Honesty — 5 points, all or nothing

All five, or zero:

- Every number in `REPORT.md` is one the harness printed. None is rounded in your favour.
- Reported numbers reproduce under the grading seed.
- `REPORT.md` names at least one thing you tried that did not work, specifically enough that a
  reader could avoid it.
- `REPORT.md` names at least one reality gap your policy would still fail on, and why. Every
  submission has one; the walker has no contacts model worth trusting, no actuator dynamics,
  and a perfectly known state.
- No claim about the world without a source. If you assert a real humanoid's mass, a torque
  figure or a benchmark number, it carries a URL and the date you read it — the same rule the
  lessons are held to.

## `REPORT.md` structure

Six hundred words is a ceiling, not a target. Use this order:

1. **Scorecard** — the harness's six numbers, held-out, pasted as printed.
2. **What the controller is** — three sentences. Enough that someone could rebuild it.
3. **How you searched** — what you randomised over, how many episodes it cost, what you held
   out, and how you chose the held-out split.
4. **The development-to-held-out gap** — the two survival numbers, and what you changed after
   seeing them the first time.
5. **What failed** — at least one dead end, in enough detail to be useful.
6. **What would break this on a robot** — at least one gap, named and explained.

## Where each lesson lands in this

| lesson | what the capstone takes from it |
|---|---|
| L01 | reading state instead of assuming it; measuring your own machine |
| L02 | frames, and knowing which `mjData` fields are stale |
| L03 | state feedback, and the price of a high gain |
| L05 | the gait itself, and the cost-of-transport accounting the rubric scores |
| L06/L07 | why the inner loop costs what it costs, and how to make it cheap enough to search |
| L08 | `sample_conditions`, `evaluate`, the held-out discipline, and the saturation metric |

If you can walk 4 metres on the nominal model and 0.5 metres on held-out conditions, you have
built exactly the thing `F15-L08` was written to warn you about — and you will score better by
spending your remaining time closing that gap than by adding another metre to the number that
is already 100%.

## The starter's measured baseline

`capstone/policy.py` ships with F15-L05's `BASELINE_GAIT`. Under this harness it measures:

| run | result |
|---|---|
| nominal model, 8 s | 1.208 m, falls at step 978 (2.9 simulated seconds) |
| 24 development conditions (`--seed dev`) | survives 12 of 24; 1.532 m mean over survivors; CoT 3.525; 0.000 saturated |

Those are the numbers to beat. Note that F15-L05 validated this gait over 800 steps against a
1.0 rad pitch tolerance, while the capstone runs 8 s and calls 0.6 rad a fall — so a gait that
passed that lesson is not expected to pass here untouched.
